The meaning behind the celebration

A story of courage
and renewal

Durga Puja is a major Hindu festival dedicated to Goddess Durga. In a well-known account from Hindu tradition, Durga defeats the buffalo demon Mahishasura, representing the victory of righteousness and balance over destructive forces.

The festival is especially prominent in West Bengal and across eastern India, and is celebrated by communities around the world. For many, it is both a sacred occasion and a joyful homecoming filled with art, music, food, and time together.

At its heart: devotion, resilience, compassion, and the bonds that bring a community together.

From welcome to farewell

The festival, day by day

The main observances usually run from Shashthi to Dashami. Customs and schedules vary between regions, families, and communities.

01

Mahalaya

The beginning

Mahalaya marks the start of the festive season for many people. Prayers, stories, and music welcome the arrival of the Goddess.

02

Shashthi

Welcoming the Goddess

The decorated pandals open, and communities welcome Durga and her children with ceremonies and visits.

03

Saptami

Rituals begin

Puja and offerings continue. In some Bengali traditions, the Nabapatrika is ceremonially prepared and worshipped.

04

Ashtami

A day of devotion

Many devotees offer Anjali, or a prayerful offering of flowers. Sandhi Puja is observed at the transition between Ashtami and Navami.

05

Navami

Celebration continues

Special prayers and community celebrations honour the Goddess. Many pandals welcome visitors throughout the day and evening.

06

Dashami

Farewell and new beginnings

Vijaya Dashami marks the farewell to the Goddess. In many places, images are immersed in water, followed by greetings and wishes for a good year ahead.

The exact observances and names can differ by tradition; local community guidance is the best source for event timings.

More than a ritual

Traditions that bring us together

⌂

Pandals & pratima

Temporary pandals become gathering places, often featuring imaginative design. The pratima, or sacred image, is central to worship and community life.

♫

Music & dhunuchi

Drums, devotional songs, and the rhythmic dhunuchi dance add to the festive atmosphere. Performances and cultural programmes vary from place to place.

♡

Food & hospitality

Families and community kitchens share festive meals and prasad. Menus differ, but the joy of welcoming guests is a familiar part of the celebration.

✿

Art & craftsmanship

Artists, sculptors, decorators, and craftspeople bring each season to life, showcasing local materials, skills, and creative ideas.

A thoughtful celebration

Make room for everyone

Whether you are visiting a pandal, joining a prayer, or celebrating at home, a little care can make the festivities more welcoming and meaningful for all.

  • Follow local guidance and be considerate during prayers.
  • Choose accessible routes and leave space for others.
  • Support local artists, makers, and community organisers.
  • Dispose of waste responsibly and respect immersion guidelines.
